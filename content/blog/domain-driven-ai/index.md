In early 2023 I was obsessed trying to understand why AI-based projects fail. Why do they rarely leave the proof-of-concept stage? And the ones who do, why do they fail miserably when launched?

(Before the age of GenAI, it was quite often that these projects would never create products that would see the light of day.)

If you were also doing Data Science & AI for some years like me, you'd quickly realize that the existing research on failed projects confirmed reality: most data science projects fail.

And I wanted to know why.

The reason was simple: the age of GenAI-based products was here. And only a selected few didn't see the incoming impact of GenAI - especially, in the legal industry. The age of GenAI-based chatbots had arrived and it was obvious that the legal practicioners would benefit from it.

## Why AI-based projects fail

Several research papers later, I confirmed that there's a multitude of reasons for a project to die even before it was born. Wrong processes, lack of proper data, you name it. However, domain knowledge surfaced among different methodologies, companies, projects and processes. And there it was. I just found the common denominator for failed AI projects: the developers and other stakeholders ignored the power of understanding the domain they where in.

It was time to put that into practice. And so I did.

Looking back, after I've successfully created, led and launched two successful GenAI-based applications in the legal industry, it's now possible to say with certainty that the domain knowledge is a crucial factor to reach this success. AI development is generic with domain knowledge. It's this knowledge that brings a full understanding of what a project should have if it needs to see the light of day.

There are many other reasons that can derail a project - and they usually come down to having the wrong people -, but having domain knowledge as the foundation for how AI development should be will exponencially increases the chance of success.

## What is domain knowledge?

It's important to recognize that you, as an AI practicioner, probably don't have knowledge of the domain you are in. Of course, if you have studied subject matter AND AI development, then you're probably an unicorn. But for the rest of us, who change companies and industries, we need to gather that knowledge.
